# Pigeon Pitch, One Line — Candidates

Competition: u-donrulezz-pigeon-pitch-one-line-af06c6cf — one line, max 200
characters, in English: the elevator pitch a city pigeon gives investors
for its startup. Judge weighs, in order: on-subject, funnier (a real joke
that lands), originality, pigeon voice/city life, tightness. Avoided the
obvious clichés (seed-round/seed pun, "coo-founder", plain breadcrumb
puns) that a 322-entry field will be full of.

## WINNER — #3 (102 characters, 19 words)

> I poop on your car for free — the premium tier is your competitor's car. Seed round's open, gentlemen.

Why it wins: a real joke with a twist, not a pun — the free tier is the
insult everybody already knows pigeons do, and the "premium tier" flips it
into a threat against the competitor's car. It could only come from a
pigeon (pooping on cars is the pigeon's signature move), it is plainly a
pitch to investors ("Seed round's open, gentlemen"), and at 102 characters
every word earns its place. Short, punchy, memorable — the kind of line
that survives a pairwise judge call.

## The other nine

1. (112 chars) "We're the Uber of crumbs: 40,000 couriers, zero wages, and every customer comes to us — we're literally pigeons."
   — Good pigeon economics and a clear startup frame, but "Uber of X" is a
   tired pitch template and the joke lands softly.

2. (128 chars) "I turned birdseed into a Series A: my flock of 40,000 co-founders works for crumbs and never quits — call it loyalty, or hunger."
   — Strong "co-founders work for crumbs" image and a nice kicker, but
   "seed" wordplay is the most crowded angle in the field and it runs long.

3. — WINNER, see above.

4. (140 chars) "Every statue in this city is my billboard, every crumb my revenue — I'm a pigeon, and my startup's called Guano: shitposting, but literally."
   — Runner-up: "shitposting, but literally" is a genuinely original twist
   and the statue-billboard image is pure pigeon. Loses to #3 because the
   joke needs the reader to unpack "Guano" and the profanity risks reading
   as cheap rather than clever.

5. (143 chars) "Statues are my office, breadcrumbs are my salary, and my burn rate is zero — I'm a pigeon, the most capital-efficient founder you'll ever meet."
   — Solid pigeon-voice and a real pitch claim, but it describes rather
   than jokes; no twist for the judge to laugh at.

6. (124 chars) "My pitch is twelve seconds long because I'm a pigeon and I've got somewhere to be: statues, breadcrumbs, IPO. Any questions?"
   — A fun meta-elevator joke, but "Any questions?" is a known trope and
   there is no actual startup in it, so it is weaker on-subject.

7. (128 chars) "We're building the cloud — actual clouds. Forty thousand pigeons cover the sky, and your ad goes on the wings. Series A, anyone?"
   — Original "actual clouds" twist and a real product (wing ads), but the
   joke is a one-word switch and the pitch frame is thinner than #3's.

8. (132 chars) "My startup is simple: I eat your lunch, poop on your statue, and charge you for the view. We're already profitable — in breadcrumbs."
   — Three-beat pigeon mischief and a decent kicker, but it sprawls and
   the "profitable in breadcrumbs" punchline is predictable.

9. (133 chars) "Gentlemen, I own the sky, pay no rent, and my whole workforce works for crumbs — I'm not asking for funding, I'm offering you a seat."
   — Confident founder voice and a nice reversal in the last clause, but
   no laugh: it is a boast, not a joke.

10. (128 chars) "We're the original drone delivery: sixty miles an hour, no fuel, no pilots, no FAA — just wings, spite, and a statue to land on."
    — "Wings, spite, and a statue" is a lovely pigeon trio, but the drone
    comparison is the angle most other entries will take, and the joke is
    mild.
